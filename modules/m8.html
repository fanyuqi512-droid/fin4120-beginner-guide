§ 2.2. Bootstrapping Methods

讲义原文

2.2. Bootstrapping Methods

2.2. Bootstrapping Methods
In real life, bonds don’t always pay at clean, regular intervals.
Bootstrapping gives a simple way to build the yield curve step-by-step
from market prices.
• We start with short-term instruments like Treasury bills to estimate
    near-term discount factors. Then we use these to isolate later
    payments from coupon bonds. We repeat this until we’ve built the
    whole curve.
解析

Bootstrapping：从最短到期券开始，逐个剥离已知票息的现值，解出下一个折现因子。

步骤：已知更短 DF → 从附息债价格减去中间票息现值 → 剩余归到期本息 → 解 DF(T)。

易错点：输入债券若含期权或流动性坑，bootstrap 会把误差一路传到长端。

Discussion

Discussion
Just "Fitting the Curve Today" Isn’t Enough!
   It’s just a snapshot. It tells you nothing about how rates might change
tomorrow or next week. – no forecast power!

Most models (like spline or Nelson-Siegel) are static — they fit today’s data
well, but:
• They don’t tell us where rates are headed
• Their parameters (like hump size) change every day in unpredictable ways
• They don’t match well with dynamic models (like Hull-White)
   that do model how rates evolve over time.

So even if your model fits today perfectly, it won’t help much for pricing
options or managing risk going forward.

Summary & Key Takeaways

Summary & Key Takeaways

• Clean Data First: Filter bonds for a reliable, tradable basket.
  Remove outliers and complex bonds.
• Direct Way - Bootstrapping: Build the curve step-by-step
  from short to long rates. Use interpolation (e.g., splines) to
  fill gaps.
• Indirect Way - Smooth Fit: Fit one smooth curve to all
  data. Nelson-Siegel is key—its parameters
  mean Level, Slope, Curvature.
• Trade-off: Direct = precise but bumpy. Indirect = smooth
  but model risk. Neither predicts the future alone.
