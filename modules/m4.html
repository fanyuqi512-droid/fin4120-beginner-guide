§ 4. STRIPPED TREASURY

讲义原文

4. STRIPPED TREASURY

4. STRIPPED TREASURY

4. Stripped Treasuries (STRIPS)
• The Treasury does NOT issue zero-coupon notes or bonds.
• However, due to the demand for zero-coupon instruments with no
  credit risk, the dealers has created such securities by coupon
  stripping.
Coupon Stripping
• Dealer strips coupon + principal into separate zero-coupon
  securities, each representing a single future payment, which can
  then be traded separately as zero-coupon bonds.
解析

STRIPS：把附息国债的每笔票息与本金拆成独立零息债。无套利关系要求：附息债价格 ≈ 对应 STRIPS 包的价格之和。

P_coupon bond ≈ Σ P_STRIP,i
易错点：若拆开后更贵/更便宜，就存在重组或拆分套利空间（还需考虑摩擦与融资）。

4. Stripped Treasuries (STRIPS)
Coupon Stripping Example:

Imagine a 10-year Treasury note with
• Face value: $1,000,
• Coupon rate: 4% (so $40 annual interest = $20 every 6 months)
• Maturity: 10 years
Then
• Total cash flows = 20 coupons + 1 principal repayment = 21 STRIPS
   – 20 separate securities for the $20 semiannual coupons
   – 1 strip for the $1,000 principal repayment

4. Stripped Treasuries (STRIPS)

4. Stripped Treasuries (STRIPS)

• STRIPS Program
   – Since 1985, authorized by Treasury; all fixed-principal notes and
     bonds eligible.
   – Book-entry system, zero-coupon, traded as "strips."
• Notations and CUSIPs

• Tax treatment:
   – Accrued interest taxed annually (even if unpaid) → negative
     cash flow before maturity.
   – Foreign investors prefer principal strips due to favorable tax
     treatment in some countries.

§ Summary & Key Takeaways

讲义原文

Summary & Key Takeaways

Summary & Key Takeaways
• Types: U.S. Treasuries include T-bills (discount), T-notes/bonds
  (coupon), TIPS (inflation-adjusted), and FRNs (floating rate).
• Safety: Backed by the U.S. government, making them virtually
  default-risk-free.
• Primary Market: Issued via single-price auctions to primary dealers.
• Secondary Market: Trade OTC 24/7; "on-the-run" refers to the most
  recent issue.
• STRIPS: Zero-coupon bonds created by stripping the coupons and
  principal of a Treasury note/bond.
• GSEs: Quasi-government agencies (e.g., Fannie Mae) have an
  implicit government guarantee and higher yields than Treasuries.
